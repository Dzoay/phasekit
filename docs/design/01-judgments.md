## Judge: rust

Independent judge: principal Rust library engineer (lens: idiomatic modern Rust, API ergonomics, type strength, simplicity, crate boundaries, minimal dependencies, compile times, semver hygiene). I re-ran every gate with CARGO_TARGET_DIR=.../scratchpad/target-judge-rust after touching all sources: host, wasm32-unknown-unknown, wasm32-wasip2 and x86_64-pc-windows-msvc check; clippy -D warnings; tests (Lean 20, Extensible 18, Kernel 13 + 1 ignored doctest, Verification-first 15). All four pass with 0 warnings and zero third-party dependencies. One exception: `cargo fmt --check` fails on Kernel with a 971-line diff.

**Winner: Verification-first.** Weighted totals (out of 230): Verification-first 173, Lean 166, Extensible 162, Kernel 152.

Why Verification-first wins:
- It scores highest on the criteria where the user was most explicit: TDD step by step, CoolProp as the oracle, and literature arbitration when bugs are found. Its compiled, cross-checked divergence register and its Parity/Corrected datasets turn 'the literature overrides CoolProp' into mechanised tests rather than prose.
- Through my lens it is also the most idiomatic and type-strong design while staying simple: one kernel crate, two traits, validated newtypes, generic flash over `?Sized` models, and lint-enforced bans on locks, env and FMA.

Lean is a close second:
- It is the simplest design, and its sketch backs its document best, including out-of-crate family integration through the registry and compat.
- It loses on performance (jets are rebuilt per term per state), type strength (public newtype fields) and lazy-loading details (eager SA; unbacked ECS-at-decode).

Extensible:
- It has the best modularity: a Gibbs solid and a value-only EOS are full out-of-tree citizens.
- It pays for that in simplicity and API ergonomics: four traits, wrapper layers, a `Composition` argument on every pure-fluid call, and duplicated `Spec`/`Input` enums.
- Its batch path also has no seam yet for lockstep SIMD.

Kernel:
- It has the best concurrency and performance engineering.
- It over-engineers v0.1 (SIMD before transport), its open seam is unreachable from the registry and facades, it leaks reducing state into the trait, and its flash and batch are unimplemented in the sketch.

The winner should adopt the grafts listed. Lean's open registry, `State::from_total` and `IDEAL_DELTA` fix Verification-first's modularity gap at near-zero conceptual cost. Kernel's precomputed B-factors, `DataSource`, lazy ECS-aware transport and the post-0.1 executor/SIMD plan fix its performance gap. Extensible's zero-core-diff second-family gate and model-supplied bracketing make the Open/Closed claim testable. CoolPropPlot (plotting, isolines, cycles) is deferred in all four, as required.

### Justifications

- **Lean**: Simplest credible design: one computing crate, one object-safe trait (`Residual`, 4 methods), zero public generics, one `Error`, private-field `FlashOptions` with const builders. The sketch backs more of its doc than any other: PT/DT/QT/PQ flash with a `verify` gate, a reference batch with per-cell `Status`, `compat::props_si_in` over any `Registry`, and the LE blob reader. tests/new_family.rs proves a vdW family from outside the crate going through Registry, compat strings, batch and 16 threads with no core edit. The Gibbs seam is only `State::from_total` (tests/gibbs_seam.rs): a solid cannot be a `Fluid`, cannot be flashed, registered or batched, and `Phase::Solid` and `IdealGas::weighted` are core edits. Performance is the weakest of the four: `PowerBlock::term` rebuilds `Jet::power(t)` and the Faà di Bruno exp-jet per term per state (eos.rs) instead of precomputing B-factors; the core is f64-only with no lane type; SIMD comes after v0.1. Type strength is softened by `Temperature(pub f64)`, with validation only at `to_molar`. Concurrency: OnceLock per fluid and a 16-thread bitwise test. However, the SA is decoded eagerly and clippy.toml bans only mul_add (no lock lint). The claim that the ECS reference is decoded with the dependent fluid is unbacked (`data::decode(bytes)` has no registry access), and it would load reference fluids even for thermo-only use. Crate boundaries are clean: data is split out, facades are thin and corrections are applied in datagen. Migration is good: compat is implemented and tested, the C legacy shim is planned, and transport is in v0.1.

- **Extensible**: Best Open/Closed story. Term enums are closed inside cprs-heos; four object-safe traits (HelmholtzModel, ThermoModel, Transport, Catalog) span families. open_closed.rs proves that a value-only Helmholtz family and a Gibbs solid (Phase::Solid), both defined outside every first-party crate, get Library lookup, flash, `Gauged` reference states and Parallel==Reference bitwise batches with zero core edits. `Gauged` removes reference-state rot for every family (map 15 X3/X7), and `rho_max` comes from the model (map 06 C10). Costs: it has the most concepts (4 traits plus Real, Jet2, Fluid, Gauged, Library, Catalog, Composition, and `Spec`/`Input` duplicating 19 arms). Crate-per-family couples every family crate's semver to the cprs-core traits. The FamilyId(&'static str) token is stringly typed. `&Composition` on every alpha/flash/state call taxes the 136 pure fluids that come first and bakes mole fractions into the contract, which materials §4 warns against (INCOMP mass fractions, humid-air ratio). `Gauged` stores a single per-component gauge, so `Gauge::mix` is silently wrong for mixtures. `Derivs2` is order-2 only. Batch runs point by point through `dyn ThermoModel::flash`, so `alpha_batch` has no caller and lockstep ρ(T,p) has no seam. Non-power kinds go through per-term AD until fast paths land. `Status` is repr(u8) in core and claimed to cross the C ABI directly. The PT flash is todo!, so walkthrough (a) is unbacked. Verification is solid: conformance suite, CoolProp800/Corrected datasets, divergence policies.

- **Kernel**: Strongest kernel engineering. The exp-of-jets engine precomputes the τ-side B-factors at decode (`PowerBlock.bt`) and adds a `Vars` δ^k table and a const ORD. `LaneKernel` executors are bitwise-equal to scalar, including a real AVX2 `#[target_feature]` entry point; unsafe is confined to one module with SAFETY comments. The registry is lock-free, uses a `DataSource`/`Blob` abstraction and loads SA and transport lazily; a counting-source test proves one decode under 16 threads. It also has the best perf-gate table, a deterministic vmath plan for cross-target bit identity, and clippy bans on f64::powi/powf/mul_add. But: (1) v0.1 ships ISA-dispatched SIMD (cprs-simd) and a partitioned lockstep batch pipeline while deferring transport and σ to v0.2. That is over-engineering against the user's 'future/investigate' framing of SIMD, and PropsSI users get no viscosity or conductivity in the first release. (2) The open seam is weaker than claimed. `HelmholtzModel::alphar(tau, delta)` plus `reducing()` forces a reducing state on families that have none (map 06 C10). `Registry`/`FluidRef` are concrete over `FluidModel { eos: MultiParamEos }`, so a PC-SAFT crate cannot reach the registry, compat or the C ABI, contradicting walkthrough (c). (3) Gibbs solids need new kernel modules. `flash()` and `eval_batch()` are entirely todo!, so walkthroughs (a) and (b) are paper only; `Lanes::exp` is per-lane scalar until vmath lands. The `FluidRef<'r>` lifetime and const-generic `[&mut [f64]; NO]` signatures add API friction. Ten workspace crates; rustfmt not clean.

- **Verification-first**: Best fit for the user's 'TDD with CoolProp as verification, source material when we find bugs', and the most idiomatic, type-strong API. It is one std-only kernel crate with module layers. Two object-safe traits take (T, ρ) and return reducing-invariant `Derivs`; flash and batch are generic over `M: PureFluidModel + ?Sized`. Newtypes validate on construction (`Temperature::new -> Result`), `Input` uses named fields, and lints deny unwrap_used/expect_used and disallow Mutex/RwLock/Cell/RefCell/env::var/mul_add. Verification is typed code: `CORRECTIONS` patches are cross-checked by id against a compiled `DIVERGENCES` register (tests/register.rs), and the Parity vs Corrected datasets make 'the difference is exactly the patch' testable. Further pieces: `Provenance::is_arbiter`, `from_printed` tolerances, and a conformance kit (fd_first_order, gauge_invariance, capability_matrix, policy_equivalence). An `IMPLEMENTED` capability set grows per milestone, so every milestone is green on Linux, MSVC and wasip2. I checked the register entries against map 13 §3 (R1234ze(E) R; Helium inconsistency) and map 10 R10 (ice VI p0 632.4 MPa). Weaknesses: `Registry` stores the concrete `Arc<FluidData>` and the compat `Session` holds `&'static Registry`, so third-party families get flash and batch but not the registry, compat or C ABI. Gibbs needs a new `GibbsModel` trait and a `Body` variant (additive core edits). `PureFluidModel` has about 10 methods. Errors are split across several types (FlashError, PropError, BatchError, CompatError). SIMD comes after 0.1 and no performance is measured. The corrections overlay lives in core rather than the data crate. The sketch backs the QT flash, a reference batch with per-cell Status, the register, and AD/FD tests; the walkthrough compiles as a no_run doctest; compat is todo!.

### Fatal flaws

- **Kernel**: The first-release scope contradicts the user's 'we must not over engineer this' and the framing of SIMD as a future thing 'to investigate'. v0.1 (M8) ships an unsafe ISA-dispatch crate, const-generic LaneKernel executors and a partitioned lockstep PH batch pipeline, while transport and surface tension (core CoolProp outputs that PropsSI users need) are pushed to v0.2. The gate's 'stop and re-plan' softens the risk, but the plan still puts optional speed ahead of required functionality.
- **Kernel**: The modularity claim is not backed by the types. `Registry` cells and `FluidRef` are concrete over `FluidModel { eos: MultiParamEos }`, so a new family such as PC-SAFT can call `flash`, but it can never be registered or reached by compat, the C ABI or WASM. Walkthrough (c) asserts otherwise. The open trait also takes (τ, δ) plus `reducing()`, which forces a fabricated reducing state on families that have none (map 06 C10). Modularity is the user's 'KEY' requirement.

### Grafts

- **from Lean**: Make the package handle hold `Box<dyn Residual>`/`Arc<dyn PureFluidModel>` instead of concrete `FluidData`, add `Registry::with_fluid(self, f) -> Registry` (a new value, never mutation), and use `compat::props_si_in(&registry, ...)` over any registry. *Why:* This closes Verification-first's main modularity gap: third-party families then reach the registry, compat, C ABI and WASM with zero core edits. Lean's tests/new_family.rs already proves the pattern; port that test as the acceptance gate.
- **from Lean**: Add a public `State::from_total(t, rho, r, m, phase, &Derivs)` now, plus the tests/gibbs_seam.rs Legendre-transform test. *Why:* It gives the states-of-matter seam now at roughly zero cost, instead of deferring every Gibbs path until `GibbsModel` lands.
- **from Lean**: Add `Derivs::IDEAL_DELTA`: the universal ρ-part of α0, so density solves use `alpha_r + IDEAL_DELTA` and never evaluate α0. *Why:* A free hot-path saving in every PT/P+X density iteration, exact for the mechanical relations.
- **from Kernel**: Precompute the τ-side B-factors per term at decode (`PowerBlock.bt`), keep a per-state `Vars` with a δ^k table, and select the order with a const ORD in the separable kernel. *Why:* It cuts per-term work in the dominant cost centre with no API change, and the existing jets-vs-AD tests still cover it.
- **from Kernel**: Add the `DataSource` trait plus `Blob::{Static, Shared}`, and the counting-source test `first_touch_initialises_once_under_contention`. *Why:* It cleanly separates embedded and fetched bytes (WASM) from the kernel, and makes 'decode exactly once under contention' a test rather than a claim.
- **from Kernel**: Keep the LaneKernel/run_lanes executor design, the cprs-simd `#[target_feature]` entry points and the perf-gate table as the documented post-0.1 SIMD plan. Activate it only when the ≥2.5× gate fires. Also add the clippy disallowed-methods entries for f64::powi/powf. *Why:* It is the cleanest side-by-side, bitwise-tested path to SIMD and keeps the physics out of the executors. Adopting it later avoids Kernel's v0.1 over-engineering.
- **from Kernel**: Use generated `fluid-<x>` data features that also enable the features of their ECS reference fluids, and materialise transport lazily, resolving ECS references through the same registry so each reference model is shared. *Why:* Subset or WASM builds can then never miss a reference fluid, and thermo-only users never load transport or reference fluids, which meets 'load only what is needed'.
- **from Extensible**: Make it a milestone gate that the second model family (cubic) lands with a zero-line diff in the core crate. *Why:* It turns the Open/Closed claim into a falsifiable, always-green check before the traits ossify.
- **from Extensible**: Get the density bracket from the model (`rho_max(t)`), tag saturation results with `SatAccuracy::{Exact, Guess, Definition}`, and apply reference states through a gauge wrapper that works for any model. *Why:* This removes fabricated bracketing metadata (map 06 C10) and makes the pseudo-pure ancillaries' trust level explicit. Reference states then work for every family, not just HEOS (map 15 X3/X7).

### Justifications

- **Lean**: The contract is a (T, rho) Residual trait that returns the reducing-invariant A_xy bundle up to order 4, so no family ever sees another family's tau/delta (map 06 C1). It has 1 public trait and 0 public generics. Both open seams are proven out of tree. tests/new_family.rs writes van der Waals in Jet2 and runs it through PT flash, batch and 16 threads. tests/gibbs_seam.rs takes a g(T,p) model through State::from_total, and I checked its transform algebra. Supporting a solid needs only an additive Phase::Solid. The power term is finite at delta=0 because powi is used instead of d*ln(delta), which matters for exact virials. The chunked eval_batch (terms outer, states inner) is bitwise equal to the scalar path and tested. Weaknesses: (1) There is a single dataset and no Parity build, so oracle rows for corrected fluids fall to a widened divergence tolerance. (2) Corrections that change EOS constants (N2 reducing density, R) leave the v8.0.0 superancillary in place, so saturation is fitted to a different EOS. The design never addresses this, although its rot table claims everything derived is regenerated. (3) Saturation is a closed core enum (None/Superancillary/Ancillary), so a family with its own saturation method needs a core edit. (4) Jet2 is f64-only, so a family written in it cannot later run on SIMD lanes without a rewrite. (5) Newtype fields are public. Performance is a plausible plan with no SIMD design beyond auto-vectorization. The scope is right: transport is in v0.1 and SIMD comes after.

- **Extensible**: This is the strongest materials and states-of-matter path. HEOS lives in its own crate, so the core never names a family. The sketch proves a value-only Helmholtz family and a Gibbs solid with Phase::Solid outside every first-party crate (open_closed.rs), and I verified its gibbs_to_derivs2 algebra. Gauged applies reference states to any family, including the solid, which designs out map 15 X3/X7. Several thermodynamic details are right: rho_max comes from the model (covolume or packing fraction, fixing map 06 C10); SatAccuracy distinguishes Exact, Guess and Definition (the last for pseudo-pure fluids); CriticalPoint carries its origin; mixtures use sparse departures with zero fractions rejected (map 04). Major weakness: the currency between families is Derivs2/Jet2 at ORDER 2 ONLY. Map 02 says order 3 is needed for first/second partial derivatives, and fundamental_derivative_of_gas_dynamics is a CoolProp output (map 01). No family can supply third-order derivatives without a trait change, critical points cannot be solved for models without a superancillary, and term checks stop at order 2 although the oracle exposes derivatives up to d4alphar. The design also has the most concepts: 4 traits, Library->Catalog->ThermoModel->HelmholtzModel layering, FamilyId tokens, and duplicated Spec/Input types. That runs against 'must not over engineer'. Every alpha call returns a Result through dyn dispatch; the cost is small but unmeasured.

- **Kernel**: This has the best performance engineering. Exp-of-jets with Bell/Stirling B-factors (algebra checked) uses precomputed tau-side factors. The SIMD lanes are tested bitwise equal to scalar, including the AVX2 entry point. The borrowing FluidRef handle avoids refcount traffic. Superancillary and transport sections load lazily. A first-touch test shows the data source is read exactly once under 16 threads. The perf-gate table is tied to CoolProp baselines, and its own msun math port aims at bitwise results across platforms. From my lens, though, the family contract is wrong: HelmholtzModel takes (tau, delta) and requires reducing(). Every PC-SAFT or cubic family must invent a reducing state (the coordinate hack that map 06 D4 says to drop), and a borrowed ideal-gas part can be evaluated at another model's tau. That is the C1 hazard map 06 warns about ('never share tau/delta between models with different reducing states'). States are built only inside the kernel's flash, and ice requires a new kernel GibbsModel trait, relations::gibbs and a new Sides variant, so states of matter are not core-edit-free (the proposal admits this). Scope is inverted: unsafe AVX2 dispatch and const-generic LaneKernel<NI,NO> executors land in v0.1 (M8), while transport and surface tension slip to v0.2. The doc also contradicts itself on reference states (in D15 they are v0.2, in M10 they are part of v0.1). eval_batch takes 9 parameters. No out-of-tree family is demonstrated in the sketch.

- **Verification-first**: This is the best verification and arbitration design I have seen for a property library. It keeps a Parity dataset (bit-exact v8.0.0) and a Corrected one, with typed CORRECTIONS cross-checked by id against a compiled divergence register (tested). Each entry gets a three-part proof: Corrected matches the paper, the oracle differs from the paper, and Parity matches the oracle. The mutable FluidRecord acts as an arbitration seam: swap one constant and re-run the paper's own table (map 13 protocol). An EOS-hash gate marks the superancillary stale and falls back to VLE after a correction, which is the thermodynamically correct handling that Lean misses. Further strengths: from_printed tolerances; arbiter versus non-arbiter provenance; t_triple kept distinct from t_min; published_critical() separate from eos_critical(); SatSource tags; SolvePath; capabilities that grow per milestone; an executed-test-count gate. The kernel follows the right (T, rho) A_xy contract, with value::<R> paper formulas, a HyperDual oracle and a Real trait ready for lanes. Weaknesses: solids need core edits (GibbsModel trait, relations::gibbs, Body::Gibbs) because State is built only by the flash. PureFluidModel ties flash to pure fluids and places transport on the EOS trait. No out-of-tree family is demonstrated: conformance_fd tests the core's own PureEos. Performance is the least developed, with SIMD only after 0.1.

### Fatal flaws

- **Extensible**: The open family currency is order 2 only: Derivs2 and Jet2 carry v, t, d, tt, td, dd, and HelmholtzModel::alpha has no order parameter. Map 02 states that order-3 alpha derivatives are needed for first/second partial derivatives, and map 01 marks second partial derivatives as P1-early (U9) and lists fundamental_derivative_of_gas_dynamics as a CoolProp output. No family can supply them, and EOS critical points for models without a superancillary are blocked. Adding order 3 later means a trait change whose default cannot be computed for dyn families, so the CoolProp-parity requirement is broken by design.
- **Kernel**: The open family contract is alphar(tau, delta) and alpha0(tau, delta) plus a mandatory reducing(). Every non-multiparameter family (PC-SAFT, cubic) must invent a reducing state, which is the coordinate hack map 06 D4 says to drop. A borrowed canonical ideal-gas part can be evaluated at the wrong tau, which re-admits the class of the C1 entropy bug. Map 06 says to exchange reducing-invariant A_nm and 'never share tau/delta between models with different reducing states'.
- **Kernel**: Future states of matter require kernel edits. A State can be built only by the kernel's flash, and ice Ih needs a new kernel GibbsModel trait, relations::gibbs and a Sides::Solid variant (the proposal admits 'not zero lines'). The user calls modularity KEY for all states of matter. The design also implements the FUTURE SIMD extension now (unsafe AVX2 dispatch and const-generic LaneKernel executors in v0.1, M8) while deferring transport and surface tension to v0.2. That contradicts 'seams now, not implementations' and 'must not over engineer'.
- **Lean**: This flaw is limited to fluids that receive corrections. The corrections overlay changes EOS constants (N2 reducing density 11183.9 vs 11183.9014...; R values per map 13), but the v8.0.0 superancillary ships unchanged. There is no stale-SA gate and no Parity dataset. QT, PQ and phase classification for those fluids then come from an SA fitted to a different EOS (about 1e-7 to 1e-6 relative). Their oracle fixtures can only be checked at a widened divergence tolerance, which hides algorithm regressions at that level. Grafting Verification-first's design fixes this.
- **Verification-first**: Gibbs-explicit solids and other future states of matter need core edits (a new GibbsModel trait, relations::gibbs and a private Body::Gibbs variant) because State is flash-only. Lean and Extensible show that a public total-bundle constructor plus an algebraic Legendre transform avoids this. The claim that a new family needs no core edit is asserted but not demonstrated in the sketch: the only HelmholtzModel implementors are the core's own PureEos and Fluid.

### Grafts

- **from Verification-first**: Two datasets, Parity and Corrected. Corrections are typed Patch/Edit values that each cite a divergence-register id, cross-checked by a test, and every entry gets the three-part proof (Corrected matches the arbiter, the oracle still differs from the arbiter, Parity matches the oracle). An EOS-hash gate marks the superancillary stale and falls back to VLE or a polish after any correction to EOS constants. *Why:* This closes Lean's main correctness gap (stale SA, no parity mode) and separates algorithm bugs from data bugs at 1e-14 for every fluid.
- **from Verification-first**: A mutable FluidRecord as the arbitration seam: decode, swap one constant for the paper's value, compile, and re-run the paper's own check table before it becomes an arbiter (map 13 self-consistency). Add from_printed tolerances (half a unit in the last printed digit), arbiter versus non-arbiter Provenance, an executed-test-count CI gate, and a rule that every #[ignore] carries a register id. *Why:* This is literature arbitration as executable TDD, which the user asked for ('coolprop as verification but with source material when we find bugs').
- **from Extensible**: Replace Lean's closed Saturation enum with an open family-trait method that returns a saturation estimate tagged SatAccuracy {Exact, Guess, Definition}. Add a model-supplied rho_max(T) for density bracketing and a CriticalPoint tagged with its origin (Numerical or Published). *Why:* Families with their own saturation methods (PC-SAFT, GERG pure fluids) plug in without core edits. Pseudo-pure dew/bubble conventions become explicit, and invented bracketing metadata (map 06 C10) is avoided.
- **from Extensible**: Add Phase::Solid now. Apply reference states (the gauge) uniformly to any model through one wrapper, tested on a Gibbs solid. Add a family conformance suite every implementor inherits: FD consistency in dimensional variables, part additivity, and thread and batch invariance. *Why:* This makes Lean's from_total seam fully core-edit-free for solids and removes the six gauge mechanisms of map 15 for every family, not just multiparameter EOS.
- **from Kernel**: Use Bell/Stirling exp-of-jets with tau-side B-factors precomputed per term as the scalar fast path. Add a counting DataSource test proving one decode under 16 racing threads, lazy SA and transport sections, a ModelKey content hash stored in State (refusing foreign states, and serving as the future surrogate cache key), an own msun exp/ln as the determinism choke point, and the perf-gate table with CoolProp baselines. *Why:* These give measurable performance and memory targets and cross-platform bitwise determinism without adopting Kernel's SIMD machinery before it is needed.
- **from Extensible / Verification-first**: Make Jet2 and each term's value form generic over a crate-private Real trait (f64, jets, later lanes), keeping zero public generic parameters. *Why:* A family's alpha^r written once in Jet2 (PC-SAFT, user EOS) can later run on SIMD lanes without a rewrite. This answers the user's side-by-side SIMD follow-up and costs almost nothing now.

## Judge: systems

Independent judge, high-performance systems lens: concurrency under parallel load, lazy loading and memory, batch throughput, SIMD and side-by-side execution, WASM (browser and WASI) and Windows. I re-ran 6 gates on every sketch with CARGO_TARGET_DIR=.../target-judge-systems: check on linux-gnu (all targets), wasm32-unknown-unknown, wasm32-wasip2 (which I added) and x86_64-pc-windows-msvc, then clippy -D warnings and cargo test. All 24 runs exited 0 with 0 warnings. Test counts match the self-reports: Lean 20, Extensible 18, Kernel 13 (plus 1 ignored doctest), Verification-first 15. No wasm runtime is installed here, so wasip2 tests were compiled but not executed.

**Winner: Verification-first.** Weighted totals (weights 3,3,3,3,3,3,2,2,1, out of 230): Verification-first 174, Lean 171, Extensible 169, Kernel 164. The top two are within judgement noise.

**Why Verification-first narrowly wins.** It best serves the user's explicit TDD contract ('using coolprop as verification but with source material when we find bugs'). Its typed, test-cross-checked divergence register and capability-gated always-green milestones are its key mechanisms. It also stays fairly simple: one kernel crate and two traits. It is the best base for the systems grafts: its Real trait already has Mask/select, and ExecPolicy plus policy_equivalence is the right harness to admit Kernel's lane executors bitwise.

**Lean, the close second.** It is the simplest and has the better Open/Closed registry. Its scores suffer on performance (f64-only, array-of-structs batch output, auto-vectorisation hope) and slightly on memory (eager ECS decode).

**Extensible.** Most modular, with Gibbs solids and gauges proven for any family. It pays for that in simplicity on every call (Composition, dyn layers, 4 traits), and its WASM runtime loading needs serde_json.

**Kernel.** Best through my lens: lock-free borrowed handles, section-lazy loading, a tested AVX2 lane path bitwise equal to scalar, and an explicit WASM memory policy. It loses on modularity: its concrete registry contradicts its own Open/Closed walkthrough. It also loses on simplicity (10 crates, const-generic executors) and defers transport.

**Recommended composite.** Verification-first as the skeleton, with:
- from Kernel: the LaneKernel/cprs-simd execution layer, the generic accumulate<R, ORD>, DataSource/Blob with section-lazy SA, transport and ECS, borrowed lookups, and perf gates;
- from Lean: the open with_fluid registry over dyn families, and from_total;
- from Extensible: a flashable Gibbs model and Gauged reference states, plus the conformance and zero-diff gates.

All four sketches built cleanly for Linux, Windows MSVC, wasm32-unknown-unknown and wasm32-wasip2 in my re-run. Remaining shared weaknesses:
- Determinism across targets depends on an in-house or libm exp/ln that every sketch currently forwards to std.
- The thread tests are not gated off wasm.
- No proposal has measured a single performance number yet (K19).
- Plotting, isolines and cycle helpers (CoolPropPlot) are correctly deferred by all four.

### Justifications

- **Lean**: Modularity: the Registry holds Fluid = Arc<Core> with a Box<dyn Residual>, so a third-party family gets name lookup, compat strings, batch and 16-thread sharing with no edit. tests/new_family.rs proves this from outside the crate. The public State::from_total is a tested Gibbs seam, but a Gibbs or non-Helmholtz model (IF97, INCOMP, ice) cannot be flashed, batched or registered until a future PhaseModel exists. IdealGas::weighted and Phase::Solid are named core edits. Simplicity: best of the four (1 computing crate, 1 trait, 0 public generics, 1 error type). Concurrency and memory: one OnceLock per fluid, no hot-path locks, Send+Sync and size asserts, and a passing 16-thread bitwise test. Weaker points: ECS references are decoded eagerly together with the dependent fluid, which violates 'only load what is needed' (a PH flash of an ECS fluid also decodes R134a or Propane). The superancillary has no separate lazy cell (deliberate and justified). The lint config bans only mul_add (no disallowed Mutex or Cell types), and every lookup clones the Arc. Performance: weakest. The kernel is f64 only with no Real trait. eval_batch puts terms outer and states inner and is bitwise-tested, but it writes array-of-structs Derivs ([f64;15] per state) through std exp with a runtime-order loop, so the hoped-for auto-vectorisation is unlikely. Explicit lanes are a post-v0.1 refactor behind a gate (honestly self-scored). Correctness: re-observed oracle values for every milestone, 6 truth layers, and a divergence register that fails when an entry goes stale. Types: public newtype fields such as Temperature(pub f64) soften 'invalid states unrepresentable' (acknowledged). Cross-platform: from_owned exists for WASM fetch and the per-fluid features allow subsets.

- **Extensible**: Modularity: best. The open Catalog lets a family register by token. ThermoModel lets non-Helmholtz backends flash, and open_closed.rs proves this with a Gibbs solid that flashes PT, takes Phase::Solid, batches and accepts Gauged reference states, all out of tree. Gauged applies reference states uniformly to every family (map 15). The planned conformance suite and M11 zero-core-diff gate keep this honest. Simplicity: the cost of that openness. There are 4 traits plus Real/Jet2, Library, LibraryBuilder, Catalog, Fluid and Gauged, two dyn layers per flash, and &Composition in every signature even for pure fluids, with Input and Spec duplicating 19 arms. Concurrency: one OnceLock slot per fluid, the superancillary in a second lazy cell, disallowed-types, and composition per call so one Arc serves many concurrent mixture compositions. Against that, the sketch's lookup allocates (to_ascii_lowercase, a Vec collect, a linear catalog scan), and the compat mixture-string cache takes a lock (facade only). Performance: Real with Mask/select exists and alpha_batch is an overridable seam. But the HEOS fast path PowerBlock::accumulate is f64 only, so lanes need a generic rewrite. alpha_batch writes &mut [Result<AlphaSplit, _>] (array of structs plus a discriminant), which is unfriendly to lanes, and lanes are post-v0.1 (M12). The test 'Parallel == Reference bitwise' is vacuous in the sketch because Parallel runs sequential chunks. Cross-platform: the binary catalog is static only (FluidEntry.blob: &'static [u8]), and runtime or WASM-fetched data goes through the json feature (serde_json). The browser lazy-fetch path is therefore the heavy one, despite dependencies.md:522 on data-segment memory. Correctness: two datasets, divergence policies and concrete check values. Rot elimination: strongest on gauge and cross-model rot (map 15 X1-X12, map 06 C5/C10).

- **Kernel**: Concurrency and memory: best. The borrowed FluidRef<'r> means a per-request lookup does no Arc refcount write (kernel-performance line 68 counts Arc::clone as a shared write). DataSource and Blob::{Static,Shared} keep the kernel free of fs and data, the superancillary and transport sections materialise in their own OnceLocks, and ECS references resolve lazily through the same registry, so a PH flash on R143a does not load R134a. clippy disallows Mutex, RwLock, Cell and RefCell, a 16-thread cold-fluid test reads the source exactly once, and preload() serves warm-up. Performance: best. LaneKernel separates what is computed from how, run_lanes<W> is bitwise equal to scalar, and cprs-simd confines unsafe to one #[target_feature] AVX2 entry point, which is exercised and bitwise-tested on this machine. The term kernel accumulate<R: Real, const ORD> is generic, so lanes do not duplicate the physics. The design also covers a partition-then-lockstep batch, a perf-gate table and zero-allocation tests. Not yet real: vmath forwards to std, and eval_batch, run_parallel and the lockstep Newton are todo! (the lockstep Newton's complexity is acknowledged). Cross-platform: best. There are two explicit wasm builds (baseline and simd128), ISA dispatch compiles on every target, the cross-target result hash is the default goal, and the WASM data-segment memory risk is explicitly mitigated. Modularity: weak, with an unsupported claim. Registry cells and FluidRef are concrete over FluidModel, so a new family can be flashed through flash<M> but cannot be registered or named, or reached from compat, the C ABI or WASM, without kernel edits. Walkthrough (c) claims otherwise, and solids need new kernel modules. Simplicity: 10 workspace crates, const-generic [&mut [f64]; NO] executor signatures and lifetime-carrying handles is heavy before the M8 gate pays for it. Correctness: SIMD (M8) comes before the full pair coverage (M9), transport and surface tension slip to v0.2 (which also weakens PropsSI migration), and the rot table is narrower (19 items).

- **Verification-first**: Correctness: best. A compiled, typed DIVERGENCES register is cross-checked against CORRECTIONS by a test (use-paper entries must name an arbiter), with three-part proof tests and a ResolvedUpstream lifecycle. It also has from_printed() tolerances, provenance-ranked arbiters with a self-consistency check, and a capability constant that grows per milestone, which keeps the 19x2 matrix green at every step. A conformance kit (FD, gauge invariance, policy_equivalence) applies to any model, and an executed-test-count gate guards CI. Concurrency: one OnceLock per fluid, a lazily materialised superancillary, clippy bans on Mutex, RwLock, Cell, RefCell and env::var, plus preload() and loaded() introspection. However, the sketch has no thread test, the doc's lazy transport cell is eager in the sketch (Option<TransportData>) with no registry path to resolve ECS FluidIds, and resolve() is a linear scan in the sketch. Performance: Real already has Mask/select, which makes it lane-ready, and residual_batch plus ExecPolicy are the seams. But only value::<R> is generic (the jet fast path is f64), Derivs is a 25-slot array-of-structs (208 B) value, Parallel returns PolicyUnavailable, and lanes come after 0.1 (honestly self-scored). Modularity: two object-safe traits, generic flash and batch, and an inherited conformance kit. But the Registry is concrete over Arc<FluidData> and the compat Session is hardwired to Registry::embedded(), so a new family cannot reach the string, C or WASM facades without core edits, and Gibbs needs additive kernel modules. Cross-platform: include_str! fixtures let the corpus run on wasip2 without filesystem mapping, a libm hash CI is planned, and from_bytes is in place. Migration: a separate compat crate with an explicit FillPolicy and Session, and a string-keyed C ABI.

### Fatal flaws

- **Kernel**: Requirement gap (fixable, not unsound): Registry stores Arc<FluidModel> and FluidRef borrows &FluidModel, so a new model family (walkthrough c, PC-SAFT) cannot be registered, named, or reached from compat::props_si, the C ABI or WASM without kernel edits. The doc claims these 'all work immediately through &dyn HelmholtzModel', and the sketch does not back that. This undercuts 'modularity is KEY / new families without core edits' at the user-facing layer.
- **Verification-first**: Requirement gap (fixable, not unsound): the Registry is concrete over Arc<FluidData>, and the cprs-compat Session is hardwired to Registry::embedded(), so a third-party family cannot reach the string, C or WASM facades without core edits. The doc's lazy transport OnceLock is not in the sketch (Option<TransportData> is eager), and FluidData::compile has no registry handle, so ECS FluidId references have no resolution path.
- **Lean**: Minor requirement deviation (not unsound): ECS reference fluids are decoded eagerly whenever a dependent fluid is decoded, so a request that needs no transport still loads Propane, R134a or N2. That conflicts with 'only load what is needed for the requests coming in'. The fix is a lazy transport cell that resolves references through the registry (as Kernel does).

### Grafts

- **from Kernel**: Lane execution layer: LaneKernel<NI,NO> (what) vs run_scalar / run_lanes<W> / fixed-chunk run_parallel (how), with a separate cprs-simd crate that confines unsafe to #[target_feature] entry points selected once at run time. Every level is forced in CI and asserted bitwise equal to scalar, and wasm simd128 ships as a second build. *Why:* This is the only proposal with a working, tested path to SIMD side by side with the scalar reference (the user's follow-up request). It slots directly into the winner's ExecPolicy and policy_equivalence differential tests without duplicating physics (K8, K9, K11, K15, K17).
- **from Kernel**: Make the hot term kernel itself generic, as accumulate<R: Real, const ORD: usize>, with per-state precomputed delta powers. Lanes then run the B-factor fast path rather than only the value::<R> AD form, and the derivative order is a compile-time loop bound. *Why:* The Real-generic code in Verification-first and Extensible covers only the AD/value form. Without this graft, lanes would need a second hand-written fast path.
- **from Kernel**: DataSource with Blob::{Static, Shared(Arc<[u8]>)}. Validate the whole blob once at decode but materialise the EOS only, and give the superancillary and transport their own OnceLocks. ECS references resolve lazily through the same registry, so the dependency is a declared DAG that is never loaded unless transport is used. *Why:* This gives the tightest 'load only what is needed' (map 11 U2/U7/U8, map 05 R7) and one clean path for browser fetch and native files without a JSON dependency.
- **from Kernel**: A borrowing lookup (FluidRef-style, or a registry getter returning &Fluid) for per-request name resolution, plus a perf-gate table with a counting-allocator zero-allocation test and a 1..N thread-scaling harness on same and different fluids. *Why:* PropsSI-style high-frequency calls from many threads on one fluid otherwise do an Arc fetch_add and fetch_sub on one shared cache line per call (kernel-performance line 68). The gates turn the concurrency claims into measured facts (K19).
- **from Kernel**: WASM memory policy: never ship all 136 fluids as the only browser build. Provide a fluids-core embed plus runtime-fetched binary blobs, and give fluid-<x> features that pull in their ECS references automatically. *Why:* Active data segments are copied into linear memory at instantiation (dependencies.md:284/522), so per-instance memory is set by what is embedded.
- **from Lean**: An open registry over a dyn-family package. Registry::with_fluid returns a new value, and Fluid wraps Box<dyn Residual>, so third-party families get name lookup, compat strings, batch and the facades. An out-of-crate test (vdW written in Jet2) proves it, including 16-thread bitwise sharing. *Why:* This closes the Open/Closed gap that both Kernel and Verification-first leave in their concrete registries, with one trait and no extra concepts.
- **from Lean**: A public State::from_total over the family-neutral total order-2 bundle as the states-of-matter seam, tested with a Gibbs g(T,p) through an algebraic Legendre transform. Also the chunked eval_batch (64-state stack chunks, terms outer and states inner, bitwise equal to scalar) as the immediate batch step before lanes. *Why:* It is the cheapest proven seam for solids and gives a zero-risk batch speedup structure that later hosts lane kernels.
- **from Extensible**: When the first non-Helmholtz CoolProp backend lands (IF97, INCOMP, ice Ih), make it a flashable model behind the same batch and registry paths (ThermoModel-style), and apply reference states through one Gauged wrapper for every family. Add Phase::Solid now. *Why:* These are proven out of tree in open_closed.rs. They remove CoolProp's six gauge mechanisms (map 15 X3/X7) and let solids use batch, compat and C without core edits.
- **from Extensible**: A family conformance suite that every family runs (FD consistency, part additivity, thread and batch invariance), with the second family's PR gated on a zero-line core diff (the M11 cubic gate). *Why:* This makes 'modularity without core edits' a CI-enforced property instead of a claim.
- **from Verification-first**: A compiled, typed DIVERGENCES register cross-checked by test against the data CORRECTIONS, plus a per-milestone IMPLEMENTED capability constant, include_str! fixtures so the corpus runs on wasip2, and an executed-test-count gate. *Why:* These are already in the winner. Keep them as the backbone that every graft above must pass through.
- **from Kernel**: Shared fix for Lean, Extensible and Kernel (Verification-first has no thread tests yet): cfg-gate the std::thread::scope tests off target_family = "wasm", or run them only on native. Then the 'full suite under wasmtime on wasip2' CI claim can actually hold. *Why:* None of the sketches gate their thread tests. wasip2 has no thread spawning, so the claimed wasip2 test job would fail as written.
